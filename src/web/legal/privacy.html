What we collect and why

  • Your account. Your email address, and your name if the service you sign in with shares it; which service that was; what you write when asking for an account; when you asked, when it was decided and by whom; when you last used hunt; when you agreed to the terms of use; and, if you use hunt’s command line, the ID of your access token. We use it to give you an account and keep your data to you. Our lawful basis is the contract between us (the terms of use); deciding who gets an account is our legitimate interest in running hunt responsibly.
  • What you keep in hunt. Your applications, companies, job postings, stages, notes, interviews and other steps, offers and pay, files you upload (a CV, say), tags, settings, and the log of changes. This is the service itself: we store it and show it to you, and nothing else. Our lawful basis is the contract between us. We don’t look at it, except when you ask us to help, when we must to fix a fault, or when the law requires it.
  • People you add. You may note recruiters, interviewers and other contacts: names, roles, email addresses, phone numbers and profile links. You decide what goes in; we hold it only for you, privately, to help your job search. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in providing the tool you use. If you think you’re in someone’s hunt and want to know more, email us and we’ll help.
  • Signing in and security. When you sign in, the sign-in services below and Cloudflare, which protects and connects hunt, process your IP address, browser details and the times you sign in, to keep hunt secure. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in keeping hunt and your data safe.

hunt doesn’t ask for sensitive information, such as about your health, beliefs or background. If a file or note of yours contains some, it is held like everything else: private to you, and only to run hunt for you. No decision about you is made by automated means: people decide who gets an account.

Signing in

You sign in to hunt through Cloudflare Access, with one of: LinkedIn, GitHub, Facebook or Google. Your account with that service is governed by its own privacy policy. When you sign in, it tells Cloudflare, and Cloudflare tells us, your email address and, if it shares it, your name. We never see your password.

Cookies and storage

hunt uses only what it needs to work, so it doesn’t ask for consent and shows no cookie banner:

  • Signing in. Cloudflare Access sets cookies (such as CF_Authorization) that keep you signed in for your session and protect hunt. They are strictly necessary.
  • Your preferences. hunt saves a few display choices you make, such as hidden columns and how much of your archive to show, in your browser’s local storage. They never leave your device, and we can’t see them.

There are no analytics, advertising or social media cookies. hunt’s typefaces are served by hunt itself, not by a font service.

Who we share it with

We don’t sell your data or share it for marketing. It is processed by:

  • Cloudflare, which signs you in, protects hunt and carries its traffic, under a data processing agreement with us.
  • The sign-in service you choose, as above. It acts on its own account, under its own policy.

hunt itself runs on servers we operate{{hosting_in}}. To do what you ask, hunt’s server also reaches a few other services, without sending anything about you: when you import from a link, it fetches that page (a job posting, say) and sends only its address; company logos come from logo.dev, which receives only the company’s web address; and exchange rates come from Frankfurter, which receives nothing about you.

We would disclose data to authorities only where the law requires us to.

Outside the UK and EU

Cloudflare and the sign-in services may process data outside the UK and the European Economic Area, including in the United States. Where they do, it is protected by adequacy regulations (such as the UK–US data bridge and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework) or by standard contractual clauses and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and what you keep in hunt: for as long as you have an account. When it’s deleted, by you, at your request or because hunt closes, everything is removed from hunt at once, and from our backups as they are replaced.
  • A request for an account that we turned down: up to twelve months, so we recognise a repeat request, or less if you ask us to remove it.
  • Cloudflare’s sign-in records: as long as Cloudflare keeps them for us, which is a short time.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law (the UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and the EU GDPR), you can ask us for a copy of your data, ask us to correct or delete it, object to or restrict how we use it, or ask for it in a portable format. You can download a copy of everything in your hunt yourself, at any time, from Settings → Backup, and delete your account and everything in it from Settings → Your account. For anything else, email us and we will respond within one month.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113, or, in the EU, to the data protection authority where you live or work. We would appreciate the chance to put things right first.

Keeping it safe

This notice covers only hunt as we run it: if you use a copy run by someone else, whoever runs it is responsible for your data there, and if you run your own, your data stays with you, and we have no access to it.
